depth cameras on qnnt: fake sensor -> driver -> node -> viewer
==============================================================

The same four layers as examples/lidar, for Stereolabs stereo cameras.

    ZED X    1920x1200 at 30 fps, GMSL2
    ZED 2i   1920x1080 at 30 fps, USB 3.0, plus an IMU, barometer and magnetometer

ONE driver for both, where the LiDAR needed two. That is what the seam says: an RS-16 and a
Helios speak genuinely different wire formats, so they get different parsers. Two ZEDs are
the same two SDK calls returning the same two planes, differing in resolution, baseline,
field of view and which extra sensors exist -- parameters, so they live in a table.

    fake_zed.zig   a process that produces frames at the camera's NATIVE resolution
        |           SHARED MEMORY: a ring of frame slots, as the SDK hands over a pointer
        |           shm.zig -- no qnnt, no sockets, no serialisation
        v
    zed.zig        the driver: parses, reassembles, validates, applies calibration
    frame.zig       the model-independent vocabulary and the deprojection
        |
        v
    camera.zig      the qnnt node: DECIMATES, packs TLD and publishes
        |           "camera/<model>/depth", "/cloud", "/calibration", "/sensors"
        v
    viewer.cpp      Qt/QML: the mono image and depth, colourised


read this part first: what is real and what is not
--------------------------------------------------

The LiDAR chain in examples/lidar fakes a wire that EXISTS. An RS-Helios pushes UDP
datagrams whose layout is documented in RoboSense's own driver, and this repository's
version of it was corrected against a real unit at 192.168.3.221 -- where almost every
guessed constant turned out to be wrong.

A ZED X has no network protocol. It runs GMSL2 coax into a ZED Link capture card and is
read through the ZED SDK:

    sl::Camera cam;
    cam.retrieveMeasure(depth, sl::MEASURE::DEPTH);
    cam.retrieveImage(left, sl::VIEW::LEFT);

There is nothing on a wire to imitate, so the handoff in shm.zig is INVENTED. It stands in
for that SDK boundary: a depth map and a mono image appearing in a buffer, which is the shape
of what those two calls hand over. Nothing about it is a ZED protocol -- there is no ZED
protocol -- and no amount of it passing tests makes it one.

What that means in practice:

  * everything from the driver's OUTPUT onwards -- frame.zig's vocabulary, the deprojection,
    the TLD schema, the node, the viewer -- is a faithful analogue of the LiDAR chain and
    would be unchanged against a real camera.
  * the fake and the driver's parser are two halves of one invention. A round trip between
    them proves they agree with each other, which is worth having (it catches offsets that
    drift) and is not evidence about hardware.
  * porting this to a real ZED means replacing shm.zig's producer with SDK calls and keeping
    everything else. That is the point of the seam being where it is -- and shared memory
    makes the port smaller than a transport would, because `takeFrame` already has the shape
    of `grab()` followed by `retrieveMeasure()`.

The LiDAR example earned a lesson worth repeating here: a fake built from the same
understanding as the parser can only confirm that understanding. Two bugs there survived
every test and fell out within minutes of real hardware.


native resolution, and where the size decision lives
----------------------------------------------------

The fake transmits at the camera's NATIVE resolution, because that is what a camera does. A
sensor does not downscale to suit its consumer. What varies is how much of a frame the
BRIDGE puts on qnnt, and that decision is `--scale`:

    --scale 1    1920x1080   186.6 MB/s    loopback only
    --scale 2     960x540      46.7 MB/s   fine on gigabit
    --scale 4     480x270      11.7 MB/s   comfortable anywhere      <- default
    --scale 8     240x135       2.9 MB/s   about what the LiDAR example moves

Those ceilings are measured, not assumed -- see BENCHMARK.txt:

    loopback, 64 KiB frames     1426 MB/s
    gigabit wired                117.6 MB/s
    an aarch64 board's link        9.3 MB/s

So 1080p whole is 13% of loopback and 1.6x past gigabit. The bridge says which side of that
line it is on when it starts, because it is the one thing `--scale` exists to choose:

    native 1920x1080, publishing 1920x1080 at scale 1: about 186.6 MB/s
      past gigabit's measured 117.6 MB/s -- loopback only

Downscaling in the bridge rather than the driver keeps the driver honest about what the
sensor sent, and rather than the fake because a sensor has no idea what its consumer can
carry.

Decimation SAMPLES, it does not average. Averaging depth across an edge invents a surface at
a distance nothing was measured at, halfway between foreground and background, and a stereo
frame is full of those edges. Sampling can miss a thin object; averaging reports objects
that are not there. It also makes the intrinsics exact: output pixel u maps to input pixel
u*scale, so fx' = fx/scale and cx' = cx/scale with no half-pixel correction.

The published intrinsics are for the PUBLISHED geometry, never the native one. Quoting
1920x1080 focal lengths for a 480x270 frame would put every point out by exactly four.


the frame size cap, which had to be raised and fixed
----------------------------------------------------

A 1080p depth-plus-mono frame is 6,220,800 bytes. The library's TLD subscriber capped a
frame at 1 MiB, so every native resolution above about 786x444 was refused outright --
regardless of frame rate, since the cap is per frame.

Worse, the cap applied to ONE END. There was no limit on publishing at all, so a producer
could emit frames every subscriber refused, and refusing is not graceful: the read loop
breaks and reconnects, straight back into the same oversized frame. One oversized publisher
produced an endless reconnect storm while looking, from the producer's side, entirely
healthy. Reproducible today:

    QNNT_MAX_FRAME_BYTES=1048576 qnnt-cat camera/zed2i/depth
    # subscribes, then receives nothing, forever

The default is now 16 MiB, which covers every native mode either camera has (2K is 8.2 MB),
and it applies when publishing as well as when subscribing -- a publish over the limit fails
at the producer, where the size was decided. `QNNT_MAX_FRAME_BYTES` sets it per node; two
nodes on a topic are two settings, so a producer raising its own does not raise its
consumers'.


the handoff, and why it is shared memory
----------------------------------------

The camera-to-driver seam went through three designs, and the last one is the only one that
models the SDK. Recorded because the wrong choices cost real work and the reasons are the
useful part.

  UDP chunking, first attempt. A 6.2 MB frame became 760 datagrams that could be lost
    individually, so the driver grew a chunk bitmap, a byte-offset scatter able to reassemble
    a depth value split across two datagrams, a `rows_missing` count, a socket receive buffer
    tuned to 8 MB, and a fix for losing 83% of every frame. Every line of it modelled a
    problem a real ZED driver does not have.

  A named pipe, considered and rejected. Ordered and reliable, so all the reassembly would
    have gone -- but macOS pipe buffers are 64 KB and cannot be enlarged (F_SETPIPE_SZ is
    Linux only). A 6.2 MB frame is 95x that, so the writer blocks about 94 times per frame
    and the two processes lock-step. A slow consumer would THROTTLE THE CAMERA, and that is
    the one thing real hardware cannot do.

  Shared memory, which is what the SDK actually is. `retrieveMeasure()` hands you a pointer
    into a buffer. A ring of slots in a shared mapping reproduces that exactly.

    header    one page: geometry, calibration behind a seqlock, sensor ring cursor
    sensors   512 slots of 64 B, drained by the consumer from its own cursor
    frames    4 slots of 6.2 MB, page-aligned, 24.9 MB mapped in total

The producer writes the next slot and stores the sequence number with a release fence. The
consumer loads it with acquire, copies the slot out, then re-checks: if the producer advanced
by a whole ring during the copy the frame is discarded as torn rather than handed over
half-overwritten. Nothing blocks in either direction.

What that buys, and all of it is the real behaviour rather than a model of it:

  * the producer never waits for the consumer. A slow driver misses whole frames and sees
    the gap in the sequence number -- reported as `missed`. That is what happens when an
    application is late to `grab()`.
  * a frame is whole or absent. There is no partial frame to represent, which is why
    `rows_missing` is gone from the schema entirely.
  * no serialisation. Depth is a native u16 array, because shared memory between two
    processes on one machine may assume a byte order and a wire may not.
  * no MTU, no fragmentation, no receive buffer, no reassembly.

Measured through it: 120 frames a second of 6.2 MB frames, which is 746 MB/s, with zero
missed and zero torn. The UDP version lost 83% at 187 MB/s.

What it gives up is that it is local only. There is no `--from` and the fake cannot run on
another machine the way fake-helios32 can. That is not a limitation of the model, it IS the
model: a camera on GMSL2 or USB is not on the network either.

One consequence caught the viewer out, and it is worth knowing about any value-shaped
handoff. The calibration is written ONCE, because it is a value the consumer can read at any
time rather than an event that repeats. Over UDP the fake resent the packet every second and
the bridge republished it as a side effect; with shared memory that accident disappeared, and
a viewer starting later subscribed, waited for the depth range it needs to scale anything,
and never received one. Keeping a topic alive for late subscribers is the bridge's job, so
the bridge now republishes on a timer and announces only on change.

a bug --scale created, and what it says about fixed buffers
-----------------------------------------------------------

`camera-viewer` crashed at `--scale 2` every time and at `--scale 3` sometimes, on both
models. The decode wrote into a fixed member array of 640x400 u16 while checking only that
the INCOMING buffers were long enough -- never that the geometry fitted the destination:

    scale 4    480x270      129,600 px     fits
    scale 3    640x360      230,400 px     fits, barely
    scale 2    960x540      518,400 px     twice past the end
    scale 1   1920x1080   2,073,600 px     eight times past

The array was a member of the object that owned the calibration and the counters, so
overflowing it corrupted the reader's own state -- which is why scale 3, sitting just inside
the bound, was unreliable rather than cleanly broken.

640x400 was correct when this chain only ever published 320x200. It stopped being correct
the moment the bridge grew `--scale`, and nothing connected the two: the constant lived in
the viewer, the new capability lived in the bridge, and no test crossed the boundary. The
buffer is now sized from the frame it decodes, the geometry is bounded against the driver's
own ceiling before anything is allocated, and every scale from 1 to 4 is checked on both
models.

    zed2i   scale 1  1920x1080    scale 2  960x540    scale 3  640x360    scale 4  480x270
    zedx    scale 1  1920x1200    scale 2  960x600    scale 3  640x400    scale 4  480x300

The general form is worth keeping: a decode buffer must be sized from the frame in front of
it, never from what the sender happened to send when the code was written.


the sensors topic
-----------------

A ZED 2i carries an IMU, a barometer and a magnetometer; a ZED X does not expose them here.
`has_sensors` on the calibration topic says which, reported by the camera rather than
assumed from the model name, so a consumer follows what the device claims.

    camera/<model>/sensors      400 Hz, about 23 KB/s -- a ZED 2i only
        t_us            u64
        model           string
        accel           [f32 x3]  m/s^2, camera frame
        gyro            [f32 x3]  degrees/s, camera frame
        mag             [f32 x3]  microtesla
        pressure_hpa    f32
        temperature_c   f32

Its own topic, because 400 Hz of 100-byte samples has nothing in common with 30 Hz of
megabytes: different rate, different size, different consumers. At 23 KB/s it is 0.2% of the
depth topic even at scale 4, which is why adding it costs nothing worth discussing.

Two honest caveats. The fake sends all three sensors together at the IMU's 400 Hz, where real
hardware runs the magnetometer at roughly 50 and the barometer at 25 -- so those two fields
are the most recent reading, not a fresh one. And the samples go out in a burst once per
frame rather than evenly spaced: the rate is modelled, the jitter is not.


the published frames
--------------------

    camera/<model>/depth        every frame, ~389 KB at scale 4
        seq           u32
        t_us          u64     the camera's clock for the whole frame
        model         string  "ZED X"
        width,height  u16
        rows_missing  u16     rows that never arrived
        depth         bytes   width*height little-endian u16 millimetres
        mono          bytes   width*height luminance

    camera/<model>/cloud        every frame, ~396 KB at scale 4, step 2
        seq, t_us, model
        points        u32     how many follow
        step          u16     every step-th pixel in each axis was taken
        xyzi          bytes   points x (f32 x, f32 y, f32 z little-endian, u8 intensity)

    camera/<model>/calibration  about 1 Hz
        model, width, height
        fx, fy, cx, cy  f32   pinhole intrinsics AT THIS width and height
        baseline_mm     f32
        depth_min_mm    u16
        depth_max_mm    u16
        from_device     bool  false means the driver's nominal defaults are in use

Depth and cloud are both published because deprojection belongs in the driver -- doing it
once beats every consumer doing it again -- but a consumer that wants the image still needs
the image. The cloud is subsampled: every pixel would be 64,000 points, 832 KB a frame,
12 MB/s. `--step` is the honest knob for that, and the `points` field reports what was
actually sent rather than the pixel count.

The intrinsics travel at the frame's resolution, not the sensor's native one. A downscaled
frame has downscaled intrinsics, and quoting 1920x1080 focal lengths for a 480x270 image
would put every point out by exactly four.

`calibration` is republished on every packet from the camera, roughly 1 Hz, and NOT only
when it changes. A consumer that attaches after the bridge would otherwise never receive it
and would wait forever -- which is what the first version did: `qnnt-cat --max 1
camera/zedx/calibration` hung, and so did the viewer. What is gated on change is the log
line, not the publish.


the geometry
------------

Depth is Z, not range. A depth map holds the perpendicular distance to the image plane, so
deprojection is the pinhole model:

    z = depth_mm / 1000
    x = (u - cx) * z / fx
    y = (v - cy) * z / fy

The fake renders with the same model inverted, which is why its ray direction is left
unnormalised with z at 1: the intersection parameter is then the depth directly, and there
is nowhere to accidentally store a radial distance that would bow the whole scene outward.

Camera axes are x right, y DOWN, z forward. The published cloud is in the same frame the
LiDAR examples use -- x right, y forward, z up -- so one world frame holds a camera and a
LiDAR together:

    world = (cam_x, cam_z, -cam_y)

The baseline is published but not used to deproject. Depth is already metric by the time it
arrives; the baseline is what bounded its precision, several steps earlier.


what the fake does that a tidy fake would not
---------------------------------------------

It drops depth. Roughly 6% of every frame has none, heavier on the dim floor, and the
pattern moves between frames rather than staying put.

That is deliberate, and it is the LiDAR lesson applied on purpose. A stereo camera has no
depth wherever it cannot match texture -- occlusions, blank walls, glare -- and a consumer
written against a gapless depth map falls over on the first real frame. A static dropout
pattern would be worse than none, because it would let a consumer that caches "where there
is no depth" appear to work.

The fake is otherwise deterministic: the same sequence number and phase render identically,
so a capture can be compared against itself.

It also reports intrinsics slightly off the driver's nominal ones -- fx 113.70 against the
default 112.x. A real calibration is never the nominal one, so a consumer that silently used
the fallback should be visibly wrong rather than accidentally right.


running it
----------

    zig build zed -Doptimize=ReleaseFast            # fake camera + bridge
    zig build zed -Doptimize=ReleaseFast -Dqt=/path/to/Qt/6.10.3/macos   # and the viewer

    ./zig-out/bin/fake-zed --model zed2i          # terminal 1: native 1080p
    ./zig-out/bin/camera-sensor --model zed2i --scale 4
    ./zig-out/bin/camera-viewer '' camera/zed2i    # terminal 3: from the project root

The mapping is /tmp/qnnt-zed2i.shm by default, created by the fake and removed when it
exits. The bridge waits for it rather than failing, so the two can start in either order.

ReleaseFast is not optional if you want native frame rate: a 1080p frame is 2.07 million ray
casts and a Debug build manages about 14 of 30. It says so rather than pretending.

What each reports:

    fake-zed: ZED 2i -> /tmp/qnnt-zed2i.shm
      1920x1080 native at 30 Hz, 4 slots of 6.2 MB, 24.9 MB mapped
      depth 200..20000 mm, fx 681.6 baseline 119.6 mm, plus a 400 Hz sensor ring
    fake-zed: 30.0 frames/s of 30 target, 9.1 ms/frame rendering, 6.4% without depth

    camera-sensor: ZED 2i from /tmp/qnnt-zed2i.shm, with sensors
      native 1920x1080, publishing 480x270 at scale 4: about 11.7 MB/s
      carryable on gigabit
      4 frame slots of 6.2 MB each in the mapping
    camera-sensor: calibration from the camera: native 1920x1080 fx 681.61,
      published 480x270 fx 170.40, baseline 119.6 mm, sensors yes
    camera-sensor: 30.0 frames/s, 60 published, 0 missed, 0 torn, 0 idle,
      388896 B depth + 395944 B cloud (+sensors)

    camera-viewer: depth range 200..20000 mm, calibration from the camera
    camera-viewer: 30 frames/s, 480x270, 6% without depth, 0 missed

The console tools read these topics like any others:

    qnnt-ls --tree
    camera/
    └── zedx/
        ├── calibration
        ├── cloud
        └── depth

    qnnt-cat --max 1 'camera/*/calibration'
    qnnt-cat --dump camera/zedx/depth > cap.tld     # and --restore replays it

Options:

    camera-sensor --model <name>   zedx or zed2i (default zedx)
                  --shm <path>     the camera's mapping (default /tmp/qnnt-<model>.shm)
                  --scale <n>      publish every n-th pixel in each axis (default 4)
                  --step <n>       and every n-th pixel of THAT in the cloud (default 2)

    fake-zed     --model <name>   zedx (1920x1200) or zed2i (1920x1080, with sensors)
                  --shm <path>     where to create the mapping
                  --slots <n>      frame slots in the ring (default 4)
                  --fps <n>        override the native frame rate

There is no `--from` and no port, because there is no network here. That is the point: a
camera on GMSL2 or USB is not reachable over IP, and pretending otherwise was what the first
version of this chain got wrong.


running it against a REAL camera
--------------------------------

    zig build zed -Duvc -Doptimize=ReleaseFast

    ./zig-out/bin/zed-uvc                          # terminal 1: the camera itself
    ./zig-out/bin/camera-sensor --model zed2i --scale 1
    ./zig-out/bin/camera-viewer '' camera/zed2i    # terminal 3: from the project root

The bridge does not grab from the camera and never did -- it reads the shared region. So
the only thing that changes is the PRODUCER: `zed-uvc` instead of `fake-zed`. The driver,
the bridge, the topics and the viewer are untouched, which is the whole point of the seam
sitting where it does.

`--scale 1` rather than the default 4: over UVC a frame is 672x376 and 22.7 MB/s, so there
is nothing to decimate. The default of 4 exists for a 1080p camera and would reduce this to
168x94.

Measured on a real ZED 2i:

    zed-uvc:       15 frames/s, gap 65.1-68.4 ms, 672x376 left eye, depth absent
    camera-sensor: 15.0 frames/s, 137 published, 0 missed, 0 torn, 758106 B depth + 62 B cloud
    camera-viewer: 15 frames/s, 672x376, 100% without depth, 0 missed

`-Duvc` is macOS only, because zed-uvc is AVFoundation capture. It is opt-in rather than
automatic so that the ordinary build needs no Xcode frameworks.


what the real camera settled, and what it could not
---------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT OFFERS IS NOT WHAT THE TABLE SAYS. The spec row for a ZED 2i reads 1920x1080 at 30,
which is the SDK's capability. Over UVC this camera offers exactly one mode:

    1344 x 376  yuvs  15 fps        = 2 x 672x376 side by side

That is all. No 1080p, no 30 fps, no choice. zed-uvc asks the camera and uses what it finds,
rather than believing the table -- the same lesson the Helios taught in examples/lidar.

And asking exposed a real bug. The driver's FALLBACK intrinsics were computed from the spec
table's resolution rather than from the geometry actually arriving, so against this camera it
produced fx 672.20 -- the focal length for 1920 px -- where 672 px needs 235.27. Every
deprojected point would have been out by 2.86x. The fake never caught it, because a fake
built from the spec table always agrees with the spec table.

Three things this cannot check, and they are not small:

  * DEPTH. It is the ZED SDK's proprietary matcher and needs CUDA, and there is no ZED SDK
    for macOS at all. Over UVC the camera hands over the raw stereo pair and nothing else,
    so the mono plane here is genuinely the camera's left image at its real rate and real
    jitter, and the depth plane is zero throughout. The chain treats that as "no depth",
    which is the normal condition for a stereo camera over much of any frame -- the cloud
    correctly collapses to 62 bytes with no points -- but no depth VALUE is exercised.
  * FACTORY INTRINSICS. They live in a per-serial calibration file the SDK fetches. Without
    it the driver falls back to nominal values and says so, which is exactly what
    `from_device` is for.
  * THE IMU, BAROMETER AND MAGNETOMETER. Also SDK-only, so the region declares
    has_sensors = 0 and the sensors topic stays silent.

So the transport, the driver, the bridge and the viewer are validated against real hardware
at real rates. Depth and calibration are not, and a Linux or Windows host with the SDK is the
only way to change that -- on which the port is replacing zed-uvc's capture with
`sl::Camera` calls that fill the same slots, and touching nothing else.

Real jitter is worth one note, because the fake does not model it: inter-frame gaps measured
65.1 to 69.2 ms against a 66.7 ms nominal period, and during startup as low as 0.6 ms, so
frames sometimes arrive in pairs. fake-zed is a metronome by comparison.
